# Capture Research Pack: user pain language

## Decision

Emphasize: fast capture that does not become a misc pile.

Capture should say this:

- Capture when the thought arrives.
- The app sorts it into an action, thread, or intention.
- Actions close.
- Threads accumulate.
- Intentions stay alive.
- Search and summary help later, but the first win is that the thought lands in the right shape.

Do not position Capture as another meeting notetaker, AI second brain, or PKM dashboard.

## Repeated pains

### 1. Fast tools create junk drawers

Apple Notes and Google Keep win because they are fast. The problem starts later. Users end up with hundreds of notes, vague titles, expired lists, unexplained links, and old scraps that are hard to use. dEssence describes Apple Notes as a dump and a junk drawer. Hacker News users also describe Apple Notes, Google Keep, email, and IM as fast capture surfaces.

Sources: [S2], [S5], [S6], [S7]

### 2. Structured tools lose at the capture moment

Notion, Obsidian, folders, tags, templates, and databases ask for decisions before the thought is saved. That makes people use Apple Notes, Google Keep, sticky notes, messages to self, or email drafts instead. Mem describes this as capture friction: where does this go, what is the title, what template should I use.

Sources: [S3], [S5], [S6]

### 3. Retrieval fails because users remember gist, not exact words

Several sources repeat the same pattern. Search fails when users must remember the exact word, folder, title, or tag. Chronicle says voice-note search turns into archaeology. dEssence says Apple Notes search works best when the query matches the words in the note. Mem gives the example of writing “budget concerns” but searching for “financial issues.”

Sources: [S1], [S2], [S3]

### 4. Voice capture solves input, not reuse

Voice notes are easy to make and hard to recover. Chronicle calls this the graveyard problem. Némos uses similar language: 200 unnamed voice memos become forgotten ideas. Otter, Noted, TalkNotes, and YouTube results show the market already understands recording, transcription, summaries, and meeting notes.

Sources: [S1], [S4], [S8], [S9], [S10], [S11]

### 5. Notes become stressful when nothing turns into action

A Hacker News commenter says ideas need periodic review and small action items, or they accumulate and cause stress. This maps directly to Capture’s action model.

Sources: [S6]

### 6. People want low-friction capture plus a slower review layer

One Hacker News user names the split clearly: an idea system should have a fast part and a slow part. They use notebook, IM, Google Keep, and scripts for fast capture, then GitHub issues/wiki/projects for slower review.

Sources: [S6]

### 7. Sync and export are trust points

GitHub issues around Logseq show users complain when sync status is unclear, login crashes, storage limits appear, custom sync breaks, assets fail to publish or sync, and setup instructions are confusing. These are not the same pain as capture, but they show what destroys trust after users store valuable material.

Sources: [S12]

## What Capture should learn

1. Lead with the capture moment, not the archive.
   - The user has two seconds, not a taxonomy session.
   - Source pattern: Apple Notes and Google Keep win because they are immediately available. [S2], [S5], [S7]

2. Keep the app’s three-shape model.
   - Action, thread, intention is sharper than folders, tags, graph nodes, or generic notes.
   - Source pattern: users fail when they must decide folder, title, tag, or template before saving. [S3]

3. Make retrieval semantic, but do not sell only search.
   - Users complain about exact-word search.
   - Capture’s stronger answer is earlier than search: shape the record when it lands. [S1], [S2], [S3]

4. Treat voice as an input, not the whole product.
   - The market already has voice recorders and meeting notetakers.
   - Capture should own “spoken thought becomes the right kind of record.” [S1], [S8], [S9], [S10]

5. Make actions visibly close.
   - The stress signal is not only “I cannot find notes.” It is also “nothing comes out of the pile.” [S6]

6. Keep sync/export boring and explicit.
   - Show last saved, last synced, export path, and error reason if Capture adds these features. [S12]

7. Use exact wording from the market.
   - Strong phrases: dump, junk drawer, guilt pile, graveyard, write-only memory, archaeology, exact words, gist, three decisions.
   - These beat abstract “second brain” language. [S1], [S2], [S3]

## What Capture should reject

1. Reject “AI second brain” as the main frame.
   - It is crowded and abstract.
   - It pushes Capture toward chat and retrieval before landing behavior is clear.
   - Inference from sources [S1], [S2], [S3], [S11]

2. Reject “meeting notetaker.”
   - YouTube and app stores heavily frame AI notes as meetings, lectures, and transcription.
   - Capture is personal thought capture. [S9], [S10], [S11]

3. Reject folders, tags, templates, and databases as the primary UX.
   - They create pre-capture decisions. [S3]

4. Reject a graph-first PKM surface.
   - The pain is not lack of a beautiful graph.
   - The pain is that thoughts do not land where they become usable.
   - Inference from sources [S2], [S3], [S5], [S6]

5. Reject “organize later” if later means manual cleanup.
   - The sources show that manual review is necessary for some people but also a common failure point.
   - Capture should sort at the moment of capture, then allow review. [S3], [S6]

6. Reject opaque sync.
   - If sync/export exists, it must be explicit and boring. [S12]

## Positioning lines to test

- “Fast capture without the misc pile.”
- “Your thoughts land as actions, threads, or intentions.”
- “Say it now. Let it land where it belongs.”
- “Not another notes archive. A place where thoughts become usable.”
- “Stop turning quick notes into a junk drawer.”
- “Voice notes should not become a guilt pile.”
- “The first job is not search. The first job is landing the thought.”
