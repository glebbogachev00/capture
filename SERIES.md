## 3 — Actions (final)

I didn't file these. I just said them. Capture turned each into something to
close and gave it a shelf life.

- "Create a project or database for collecting talks and info from people I
  admire" — fades in 21 days
- "Add entries for Jim Jannard, Jack Dorsey, Steve Jobs, and others" — 21 days
- "Resume Neuromancer audiobook at 30:29" — fades in 7 days

A call to return shouldn't outlive a week. A commitment to myself gets longer.
The model judges the shelf life from what the thing is, not from a date I pick.

And here is the part I like: if I don't do them, they go. The list stays
honest because what I ignore disappears on its own. No inbox rot.

---

## 4 — Threads (final)

Most of capture was built while I was walking or sitting. I'd talk my findings
into ChatGPT's voice, then paste them in. Each fragment joined one thread, and
the thread's "where this stands" block rewrote itself from everything I'd
said.

So the thread never described my last edit. It described the real state — every
voice note, every half-thought, folded into one live summary.

Threads don't expire. A thought that accumulates stays accumulated. One with
nothing new for two months just rests, still there, until I come back.

That is the difference from a to-do list. A list wants closure. A thread wants
mass.

## 5 — Intentions, the mechanic (final)

Most apps treat a goal as a checkbox. capture treats it as a fact.

An intention is written in present tense, as already true: "I am someone who
___." Then you name the recurring behaviours pulling against it, the ones
that usually win. Then three actions you take *because* the state is already
so, not steps toward making it so.

No due date. No progress bar. The app holds the version of you that already
is, and leaves the becoming to life.

---

## 6 — Intentions, the lineage (final)

capture's intentions feature is not a productivity trick. It is three books,
turned into software.

Reality Transurfing (Zeland): the outer world mirrors your inner
significance. Name what pulls against a goal and you bleed off its charge.

Fred Dodson (Parallel Universes of Self): somewhere a version of you already
lives the fulfilled state. You do not become them, you tune to them.

Neville Goddard: assume the feeling of the wish fulfilled. Live from the end,
not toward it.

So an intention in capture is never written as a goal. It is written as
already true. The three actions you list are things you do *because* it is so,
not steps to get there. The app does not track progress toward who you will
be. It holds the version of you that already is.

## 7 — A day in the life (final)

A real morning, three kinds, no filing by hand.

I go for a walk and voice-record what I'm learning about building the app.
Paste it in — it lands in the building thread, and the summary rewrites itself.

Later I think of something small: "resume Neuromancer audiobook at 30:29." I

And the thing holding the day together isn't a task at all. It's an intention
already written as true: "I inhabit a space surrounded by the Oakley collection

already is.

When it's worth keeping, two clicks shares any of it. The capture is mine; the
sharing is a choice.

One surface. Action, thread, intention. None of it sorted by me.
