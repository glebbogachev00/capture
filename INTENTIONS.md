# Intentions — where the feature came from

The intentions feature in capture is not a productivity trick. It is three
books, turned into software.

Gleb reads reality-creation work, and the feature is a direct translation of
it:

- **Reality Transurfing** (Vadim Zeland) — the outer world mirrors your
  inner significance. Naming the behaviours that pull against a goal bleeds
  off its charge, so it stops dragging.
- **Parallel Universes of Self** (Frederick Dodson) — somewhere a version of
  you already lives the fulfilled state. You do not become them; you tune to
  them.
- **Neville Goddard** — assume the feeling of the wish fulfilled. Live from
  the end, not toward it.

So an intention in capture is never written as a goal. It is written as
already true: "I am someone who ___." The recurring behaviours pulling
against it are named. Three actions are listed, taken from the fulfilled
state, things you do *because* it is already so, not steps to get there.

The app holds no progress bar and no due date. It is not tracking who you
will become. It is holding the version of you that already is.

## How capture came to be (source of truth)

capture was not designed up front. It emerged from a problem, and from use.

The problem: around 400 notes spread across Google Keep and Notes. Quick
captures piled up and I kept forgetting to clear them — and I did not want to
sort through 400 notes. Keep was where building ideas lived, things I kept
adding to thread-like, but nothing filed them.

An earlier tool, App Stream, had one mechanic: notes that auto-delete. It
solved the pile-up but not the sorting.

capture took that seed and added intelligence. Before it, two separate tools
existed: **Intent OS** and **Action OS**. Intent OS held declarations; Action
OS held things to close. Both were built with Claude Opus on a single $20
subscription — no dev team, one serious model.

capture launched with two of the three kinds: **Actions** and **Threads**.
(Threads also came out of the early naming as "threats" and "action idea"
before settling.) It was a place to say things and watch them file themselves.

Gleb kept using Intent OS alongside it. Once he saw he was reaching for both
tools every day, he folded Intent OS into capture as the third kind —
**Intentions**. One app, three places, no separate OS to switch between.

So the three kinds are not a feature list someone planned. They are the tools
a person actually used, merged once the use was proven — and the intelligence
that App Stream only hinted at.
